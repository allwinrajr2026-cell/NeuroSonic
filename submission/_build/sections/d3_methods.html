Methodology

Detector selection

Four candidates were run through the same benchmark harness: the previous production detector SSDLite320-MobileNetV3-Large [[ssd,mbv3]] (torchvision), RF-DETR Nano [[rfdetr]] (rfdetr 1.11.2), and YOLO26n and YOLO26s [[yolo26]] (ultralytics 8.4.198, used for the benchmark only). All are trained on COCO [[coco]].

  • Quality was measured as person average precision with the same AP code used to evaluate WOAD, on 365 near-infrared validation images containing 129 annotated people. These are the only locally available images with person boxes. They are not phone RGB images, so they rank the detectors but do not predict street performance. Precision and recall are also reported at the production confidence threshold of 0.40.
  • Latency was the mean over 200 (GPU) or 60 (CPU) recorded 640×480 frames, back to back, FP32, batch 1, after 10 warm-up frames.
  • Licence was a criterion because the system is a network service: AGPL-3.0 would oblige release of the source code to its users.

Detector runtime and fall-back

RF-DETR runs on CUDA from local weights; downloads are blocked at run time. Detections are restricted to 23 obstacle-relevant COCO classes (e.g. person, bicycle, car, motorcycle, bus, truck, dog, cow, chair, bench, potted plant) at confidence ≥ 0.40. A wrapper falls back to SSDLite on the CPU if CUDA, the package or the weights are missing, if warm-up fails, or if a run-time inference fails, in which case the same frame is re-analysed by SSDLite. Three serving changes were made after measurement: PyTorch is limited to two CPU threads while the GPU detector is active; all inference runs on one long-lived worker thread rather than on the HTTP server's per-request threads; and RF-DETR runs in FP16 without graph compilation, with FP32 as automatic fall-back.

Camera motion estimation

Up to 150 Shi–Tomasi corners [[shitomasi]] are taken from a 320-pixel grey image, outside the detected boxes so that moving objects do not bias the estimate. They are followed into the next frame with pyramidal Lucas–Kanade optical flow [[lk,bouguet]], and a similarity transform (translation, scale, rotation) is fitted with RANSAC [[ransac]] using OpenCV [[opencv]]. With fewer than 12 corners or inliers the camera motion is marked "unknown". The transform M maps a point in the previous frame to where a static point would appear now.

Motion-compensated tracking

Each track's box is predicted by applying M to its last box. Association follows the two-stage idea of ByteTrack [[bytetrack]], in place of SORT's Kalman prediction [[sort]]:

  • detections with confidence ≥ 0.55 are matched first, greedily by IoU, within each class;
  • detections between 0.40 and 0.55 may only extend existing tracks;
  • tracks end after three missed frames.

Every update stores the object's displacement relative to the background, i.e. the observed displacement minus the displacement predicted from camera motion alone:

Δrel = ct − M(ct−1)

where c is the box centre. A parked car seen while the user turns has Δrel ≈ 0, whereas a person stepping sideways has a large Δrel.

Walking-path states and events

The walking path is a lower-central trapezoid of the image. Its state is CLEAR_VIEW, POSSIBLE_OBSTACLE, BLOCKED_VIEW, UNCERTAIN (dark or blurred frame) or CAMERA_UNAVAILABLE, confirmed over 2-of-3 or 3-of-3 frames before it is spoken, with chest-mounted and hand-held wording (e.g. "The visible path appears clear. Continue carefully."). Events are considered only for confirmed tracks (at least three updates over at least 0.4 s) of people, vehicles or animals:

  • Entering: the object was outside the path at the start of a 1.5 s window, overlaps it now, and moves towards it at ≥ 0.12 image widths per second relative to the background.
  • Crossing: the object's centre passes from below 35 % to above 65 % of the image width (or back) within 3 s at ≥ 0.15 widths/s, with the bottom of its box in the lower half of the image.

Nothing is announced while the camera shifts more than 6 % of the width per frame, zooms more than 4 %, or has no motion estimate; nothing is announced in BLOCKED_VIEW, UNCERTAIN or CAMERA_UNAVAILABLE; each track triggers at most one event; and events are at least 3 s apart. An example message is "Caution. A person is moving into your path from the left." An "approaching" event was deliberately not implemented: with a moving camera, box growth is dominated by the user's own walking.

Speech, voice commands and routing

  • Speech output: Piper [[piper]] (VITS-based [[vits]]) with the Indian-English voice en_IN-spicor; results are cached and delivered as WAV with HTTP range support (needed by iOS); the phone's own voice is the fall-back.
  • Voice commands: Vosk [[kaldi]] with the small Indian-English model, in two passes (grammar-constrained, then free), combined by an intent parser: navigate to, search, start/stop navigation, repeat, start/stop awareness, status, help, test voice. Spoken destinations are only proposed and confirmed by the user.
  • Routing: A* [[astar]] with a haversine heuristic on an OpenStreetMap [[osm]] extract of Vellore (31,439 path nodes, 813 named places). Turn instructions are spoken about 70 m and 25 m ahead; the route is recalculated after two consecutive off-route positions.
  • Optional cloud description: one photo to Gemini [[gemini]] after explicit consent, with a 30 s attempt timeout, a 45 s budget and one retry for a quick "service unavailable"; while waiting, the phone says that local guidance continues.
