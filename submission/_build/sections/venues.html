1. What the Work Can Support Today

The evidence available now is: a survey; a reproducibility study of a 2025 Nature Communications system with a bit-identical re-implementation and a dataset audit; a controlled detector comparison; and a full system implementation with laptop benchmarks, soak tests and automated tests. There is no iPhone field test and no user study yet. Journals in assistive technology normally expect user evaluation, so the realistic plan is a conference paper or book chapter now, and a journal article after the validation protocol (and, for users, ethics approval) is completed.

3. Recommended Plan

  1. IPR first (now). Submit the Lab 9 IDF to the IPR cell and tell the cell that the GitHub repository (code, paper drafts) was made public on 4 November 2026, because publication before filing can affect novelty.
  2. Conference paper or book chapter (next). Adapt the Lab 10 draft as a system paper: "laptop-assisted, offline phone-based awareness with motion-compensated events, and lessons from serving a GPU at a fixed frame rate". Target: an IEEE conference indexed in IEEE Xplore, or a Springer conference published in LNNS (Q4). For a book chapter, the consolidated report already exceeds the 15-page requirement.
  3. Contact the base-paper authors (before publishing the reproducibility findings). Ask whether the labelled person/Ebike/Car data of the reported run is available. Good practice, and it may let the reported accuracy be re-measured.
  4. Journal article (after validation). Run the 23-scenario iPhone protocol; obtain ethics approval for a study with visually impaired participants; then submit to Sensors or IEEE Access, or, with a substantial user study, Disability and Rehabilitation: Assistive Technology.
