Technology Trend: Introduction and Adaptation Status

{{f-timeline}} places the surveyed documents on a timeline. Five technology trends run through them. For each we describe the technology, its state of adoption in assistive products, and how it is adapted in this project.

From range sensing to recognition

Early ETAs measured distance with ultrasound [[pat_polaroid,pat_st]], and ultrasonic smart canes are still the most widely available products [[p_wewalk,p_smartcane]]. Since about 2016, deep convolutional detectors such as YOLO [[yolo]], SSD [[ssd]] and mobile networks such as MobileNetV3 [[mbv3]] made it feasible to name objects in real time. Products like Seeing AI and Lookout show that recognition is now commercially mature for on-demand use [[p_perkins,p_lookout]]. Adaptation here: NeuroSonic detects 23 obstacle-relevant classes from the COCO dataset [[coco]] in every frame, rather than on demand.

Transformer-based detection

DETR [[detr]] reformulated detection as set prediction with transformers [[transformer]], removing the hand-tuned non-maximum suppression step. Real-time variants now exist; RF-DETR [[rfdetr]] builds on the self-supervised DINOv2 backbone [[dinov2]] and is released under the permissive Apache-2.0 licence. The base paper also uses transformer blocks, but for fusion of two modalities inside a YOLOv5 detector [[woad]]. Status: research and open-source tools are mature; we did not find a surveyed assistive product that states it uses a detection transformer. Adaptation here: four detectors were benchmarked on the same images, and RF-DETR Nano was selected for its accuracy, recall and licence (Review 3).

Multi-modal and depth sensing

Research wearables overwhelmingly combine RGB or infrared with depth [[woad,bai2019,lin2019,tang2025]], and the NOA harness brings depth cameras to a product [[p_noa]]. Depth gives distance directly, which simplifies warnings. Status: established in research, early in products, and expensive. Adaptation here: the phone camera has no depth stream that a browser can use, so NeuroSonic deliberately makes no distance statements and uses image-space motion instead; monocular depth estimation is listed as future work.

Edge, offloading and dedicated hardware

Systems differ in where they compute: on an FPGA plus phone [[woad]], on a separate processor [[lin2019]], fully on the wearable [[tang2025]], or in the cloud [[p_perkins]]. Offloading to a nearby, more powerful computer over a local network is a middle path: lower latency and better privacy than the cloud, more capacity than a phone. Status: standard in robotics and gaming, uncommon in assistive products. Adaptation here: the iPhone is a thin browser client; a laptop GPU on the same Wi-Fi performs detection, tracking, speech recognition, speech synthesis and routing, with no internet required.

Vision-language models and offline speech

Large multimodal models now describe whole scenes in natural language and answer questions about a photo, as in Be My AI [[p_perkins]]; APIs such as Gemini [[gemini]] make this available to developers. They depend on the internet and can state things with unjustified confidence. In parallel, offline speech technology has improved: Kaldi-based Vosk recognises speech locally [[kaldi]], and Piper [[piper]], built on VITS [[vits]], synthesises natural speech on a CPU. Status: cloud description is commercially deployed; high-quality offline speech is mature in open source. Adaptation here: local speech in and out is the default; cloud scene description is optional, opt-in, rate limited and filtered so that it never states distance or safety.
