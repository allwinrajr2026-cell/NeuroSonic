Introduction

A base paper is useful to a project only if its claims can be reproduced and its limits understood. This review therefore does three things for the base paper of the NeuroSonic project, "A wearable obstacle avoidance device for visually impaired individuals with cross-modal learning" (WOAD) by Gao et al., published in Nature Communications in March 2025 [[woad]]:

  1. it summarises the base system and the results the authors report;
  2. it re-implements the published detector and evaluates it on the published data, to learn its strengths and its weaknesses ("cons") from evidence rather than from the paper alone; and
  3. it identifies the gaps that prevent the base work from serving the users and settings of interest here (an ordinary smartphone, Indian streets, low cost, offline operation), and derives the proposed work from those gaps.

WOAD was chosen as the base paper because it is recent, published in a high-impact journal, addresses exactly the problem of obstacle avoidance while walking, reports strong user results, and releases its source code and data publicly. That last point is what makes a genuine re-implementation possible. All measurements in this review were made by us on one laptop (AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS, 15.3 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX 4050 Laptop GPU) between 1 and 3 November 2026; numbers reported by the authors are labelled as such and kept separate.
