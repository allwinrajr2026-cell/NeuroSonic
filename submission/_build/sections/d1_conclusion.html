Synthesis and Conclusion of the Survey

The survey answers three questions that shape the rest of the project.

Is the problem significant? Yes. At least 2.2 billion people have a vision impairment, 43.3 million were blind in 2020 and 61 million are projected by 2050, and in India about 2 % of people aged 50 and over are blind, rising to 11.6 % above 80 [[who,gbd,nbvis2]]. The population is large, ageing and largely in settings where cost matters.

What do existing solutions leave open? Research systems achieve strong results with dedicated hardware: depth glasses, FPGAs, haptic skins and robots [[woad,tang2025,cabot]]. Products split into affordable but limited smart canes, expensive wearables, and free phone apps that work on demand rather than continuously [[p_aarp,p_perkins]]. Patents cover range sensing, camera recognition with speech, and navigation narration, but the sampled patents do not describe continuous, camera-motion-compensated warnings of objects moving into the walking path from an unmodified phone [[pat_ford,pat_caltech,pat_aph]].

Which technologies are ready to use? Real-time detection transformers with permissive licences, standard multi-object tracking, offline neural speech recognition and synthesis, and open map data are all mature [[rfdetr,bytetrack,kaldi,piper,osm]]. Depth on ordinary phones and dependable cloud description are not yet ready for a mobility aid.

Direction for the project. Re-implement and verify the base paper (Review 2); then build a system that uses an ordinary phone as camera, microphone, GPS and speaker; runs perception, speech and routing offline on a nearby laptop GPU; warns about objects moving into or across the walking path while ignoring the user's own movement; and states honestly what it can and cannot see (Review 3). The system is a supplement to the white cane, not a safety device.

The main limitation of this survey is its breadth: it is a structured sample rather than a systematic review, the patent search was limited to US documents on Google Patents, and product details come from manufacturer-adjacent or retailer sources that change over time. These limits are stated so that the comparisons are read as indicative.
