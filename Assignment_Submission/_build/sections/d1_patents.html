Survey: Comparative Analysis of IPRs (Patents)

Search method

Patents were searched on Google Patents with combinations of the terms visually impaired, blind, obstacle detection, navigation, camera, audio and wearable, and in the CPC group A61H 3/06 (walking aids for blind persons). For each candidate the bibliographic record was opened and the assignee, inventors, dates, legal status and summary were read. Nine documents were retained, chosen to span four decades and the main technical families. A search of the Indian Patent Office database was not completed in this review; for example, for IIT Delhi's SmartCane we could confirm the device and its funding but not a patent number, so it appears under products instead. A professional freedom-to-operate search would be needed before any filing (see the IDF in Lab 9).

The patents

US 4 280 204 (Polaroid, 1981) [[pat_polaroid]] describes a mobility cane with a capacitive electrostatic ultrasonic transducer that both emits and receives; one mode warns of low-hanging objects at upper-body height and another detects more distant objects. It is an early example of the ultrasonic ETA family, expired long ago.

US 2005/0208457 A1 (Fink and Humayun; later assigned to Caltech) [[pat_caltech]] proposes a head- or eyeglass-mounted camera that captures an image on request, classifies the object with neural networks, look-up tables or thresholds, and speaks a description including colour and brightness. The application was abandoned. It anticipates, in a simple form, the camera-plus-speech idea of today's apps.

US 9 429 446 B1 (Searle et al., 2016) [[pat_searle]] claims a hand-held device that locates objects with a camera, radio, sonar or infrared module, plans a path and gives spoken directions. It lapsed in 2024 for non-payment of maintenance fees.

US 9 846 231 B2 (STMicroelectronics, 2017) [[pat_st]] claims a wearable obstacle detector with an ultrasonic source and receiver plus a three-axis accelerometer; the control module enables the ultrasound and the time-of-flight measurement only when the foot rests on the ground during walking, then computes the obstacle distance and raises an alarm within a given range. The idea of gating sensing by gait phase is relevant to camera systems too, because image quality and apparent motion also depend on the walking cycle.

US 9 993 384 B1 (Toyota, 2018) [[pat_toyota]] guides a user towards two destinations simultaneously with two distinct audio cues whose pitch or volume varies with the alignment between the user's heading and each path.

US 10 299 982 B2 (Frankel, 2019) [[pat_frankel]] uses a smartphone or tablet camera and orientation sensors to build a 3-D model of a room; touching a spot on a top-down view announces the object there and its attributes, such as potential hazards. Expired in 2023.

US 10 809 079 B2 (Ford, 2020) [[pat_ford]] describes a wearable garment such as a vest with GPS receivers, cameras, an ultrasonic sensor and a microphone that detect static and dynamic obstacles to generate and revise routes, with audio instructions and haptic cues, and optional upload of dynamic-obstacle data to update a map.

US 11 112 261 B2 (Brown University, 2021) [[pat_brown]] covers wearable navigation devices with multiple fixed cameras that localise the user by matching features against prior images and by visual-inertial odometry, and haptic belts or vests that render directional cues.

US 11 318 050 B2 (American Printing House for the Blind, 2022) [[pat_aph]] uses a smartphone's location services (GPS, compass, accelerometer, gyroscope) to narrate only the streets and points of interest in the direction the device is pointed, and Bluetooth beacons for indoor position and floor, with audio and haptic or braille output.

Observations from the patent survey

  • Two clusters. Patents either sense obstacles (ultrasound, cameras) or support navigation (location narration, haptic direction), with the Ford vest [[pat_ford]] as the main example combining both, on a dedicated garment.
  • Several early patents have lapsed [[pat_polaroid,pat_searle,pat_frankel]], and the camera + speech concept itself appears in an abandoned 2005 application [[pat_caltech]]; the general idea of "camera, recognise, speak" is therefore not novel and cannot be claimed.
  • What the sampled patents do not describe is the specific combination pursued in NeuroSonic: an unmodified phone browser as a thin client; camera-motion-compensated tracking that announces only objects moving relative to the background into or across a walking-path region; suppression of announcements during strong camera motion; and offline speech, commands and routing on a nearby computer. Whether this combination is patentable can only be judged after a full search; this is the subject of the invention disclosure (Lab 9).
