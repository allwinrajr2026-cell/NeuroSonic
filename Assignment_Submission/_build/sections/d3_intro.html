Introduction

Review 1 established the need for affordable, continuous, motion-aware guidance for pedestrians with visual impairment, and Review 2 showed that the base paper's cross-modal detector [[woad]] cannot be driven by an ordinary phone camera and that its reported accuracy cannot be reproduced with the published data. This review reports the implementation of the proposed system, NeuroSonic, and analyses its results.

NeuroSonic uses an iPhone as the camera, microphone, GPS receiver and speaker of a perception and navigation system that runs on a laptop over the local Wi-Fi network. Its contributions are:

  1. a working end-to-end prototype: a browser client and a laptop server with 28 HTTP endpoints, providing offline walking-path guidance, event alerts, voice commands, neural speech and offline routing;
  2. a controlled comparison of four real-time detectors on the same images, leading to RF-DETR Nano [[rfdetr]] with an SSDLite fall-back [[ssd,mbv3]];
  3. a camera-motion-compensated tracker and two conservative, rule-based events ("entering the walking path" and "crossing in front") designed to avoid false alarms from the user's own movement;
  4. a measured account of three performance problems that arise when a GPU serves a low, fixed frame rate, with before/after data; and
  5. a strict separation of what was measured on the laptop, what was simulated in a browser, and what still needs an iPhone and field tests, with a protocol for the latter.

All measurements were made on one laptop (HP Omen; AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS; 15.3 GB RAM; NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU, 6 GB) on 2–3 November 2026. No result in this review comes from an iPhone in a real street or from users with visual impairment.
