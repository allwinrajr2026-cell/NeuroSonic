Proposed Work

Problem statement

Provide a pedestrian who carries an ordinary smartphone with short, timely spoken information about recognised objects in the walking path, including objects moving into or across it, together with spoken route guidance; without depending on the internet, without storing camera images, without special sensors, and without overstating what the system can see.

Objectives

  1. O1 (real time): process phone frames at about 5 frames/s, with an answer well within one frame interval (200 ms).
  2. O2 (detector): choose the detector by measured accuracy, recall, latency and licence, with a CPU fall-back.
  3. O3 (motion): announce people or vehicles entering or crossing the walking path, but not the apparent motion caused by the user's own walking.
  4. O4 (offline): keep detection, speech output, voice commands and routing working without internet; make cloud description opt-in.
  5. O5 (honesty): never state distances or safety; say "appears clear", never "safe".
  6. O6 (verification): verify each function with automated tests, and state what is not verified.

Proposed architecture

The phone becomes a thin client running in Safari. It captures camera frames, microphone audio and GPS, and plays speech. A laptop with a consumer GPU on the same Wi-Fi network runs all engines ({{f-proparch}}). This replaces the custom glasses, FPGA and compression of the base system (G1, G7) with hardware the user is likely to have or can borrow, at the cost of depending on the laptop.

Methodology

  • Detector selection (G1–G3). Benchmark candidate RGB detectors trained on COCO [[coco]], from the established SSDLite/MobileNetV3 [[ssd,mbv3]] to the YOLO family [[yolo26]] and the real-time detection transformer RF-DETR [[rfdetr,detr]], on the same annotated person images with the same AP code used for WOAD; select by recall first, because a missed person is worse than an extra caution.
  • Camera motion and tracking (G4). Estimate the camera's own motion between frames from background corner points [[shitomasi]] tracked with pyramidal Lucas–Kanade flow [[lk,bouguet]] and a RANSAC similarity fit [[ransac]]; predict each track's box with that motion and associate detections in two stages as in ByteTrack [[bytetrack,sort]]; store each object's motion relative to the background.
  • Walking-path states and events (G4, G5). Define a lower-central walking-path region; confirm path states over 2–3 frames; raise "entering" and "crossing" events only for confirmed tracks with sufficient background-relative speed, and suppress all events during strong camera motion.
  • Speech, commands and routing (G6, G8). Offline neural speech with Piper [[piper,vits]], offline command recognition with Vosk [[kaldi]], and A* routing [[astar]] on an OpenStreetMap extract [[osm]] of Vellore, with spoken turn instructions.
  • Serving at the real frame rate (G7). Measure latency at 5 frames/s, not only back to back, and correct any serving bottleneck found.
