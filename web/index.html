            About

            Assistive research prototype. It can miss obstacles and give false alarms. It does not detect steps, poles, kerbs or holes, and it gives no distances.

            Not a replacement for a cane, guide dog or human assistance. No guarantee of safety.

            The phone is the eyes, ears and voice; the laptop is the brain. They must be on the same local network. NeuroSonic is not a standalone iPhone app. Local guidance needs no internet; only the optional online scene description does.

            1. What is NeuroSonic?
            • A research prototype that helps a visually impaired person while walking.
            • It speaks short messages about the path ahead and gives walking directions.
            • It runs in Safari on the phone, connected to a laptop on the same Wi-Fi.
            2. Why was it developed?
            • To study how cameras, speech and offline maps could support independent walking.
            • It builds on the WOAD research detector (infrared + depth images) and adds a practical phone app.
            • It is an aid to explore ideas, not a safety device.
            3. How does camera awareness work?
            • About 2–3 times a second the phone sends a small camera picture to the laptop.
            • The lower middle of the picture is treated as the walking path.
            • If a recognised object is in that area in at least 2 of 3 pictures, you hear "Caution" or "Stop".
            • "Clear" is said only after 3 good pictures with nothing in the path area, and it always means "nothing recognised", never "safe".
            • If the picture is too dark or blurred you hear "I cannot clearly assess the path".
            4. How does object detection work?
            • A small neural network (SSDLite with MobileNetV3) finds common objects: people, vehicles, animals, benches, chairs and others.
            • It draws a box around each one. The box tells left, centre or right, but not the distance.
            • It cannot see steps, kerbs, potholes, poles or walls.
            • The WOAD model needs a special infrared + depth sensor, so it is shown with recordings only.
            5. How does offline voice recognition work?
            • Tap Speak and say a command. The phone records until you stop speaking.
            • The recording goes to the laptop, where Vosk (an offline speech recogniser) turns it into words.
            • Commands such as "start navigation" work well. Place names are harder, so the app says what it heard and waits for you to confirm.
            6. How does spoken feedback work?
            • The laptop creates speech with Piper, an offline neural voice (Indian English).
            • Messages have priorities: "Stop" warnings come first and can interrupt a direction.
            • Repeated messages are suppressed and old warnings are dropped, so you only hear what is current.
            • If the neural voice fails, the phone's own voice is used.
            7. How does the offline Vellore map work?
            • Streets and places around Vellore were downloaded once from OpenStreetMap and stored on the laptop.
            • Search and routes work without internet. The map is drawn by the app itself; no map tiles are loaded.
            • Outside the map area there are no offline routes.
            8. How does GPS navigation work?
            • The phone's GPS gives your position and its accuracy.
            • The laptop finds a walking route along streets (A* search).
            • As you walk, the app announces turns about 70 m and 25 m before them.
            • If you are clearly off the route twice in a row, it recalculates. If GPS is lost or inaccurate, guidance pauses and tells you.
            • GPS is often 5–20 m off, so directions are approximate.
            9. Walking Guidance vs Object Description
            • Walking Guidance (default): only the path state is spoken — clear, caution, stop, uncertain or camera unavailable — and only when it changes.
            • Object Description: additionally names objects with their side, e.g. "Car on the right". It is more talkative.
            10. Current limitations
            • Not tested yet on a real iPhone while walking, or outdoors with real GPS.
            • Needs the laptop and Wi-Fi; stops if the connection is lost. iPhone pauses web pages when the screen locks.
            • No distances. No detection of steps, kerbs, holes, poles or walls. Objects can be missed or invented.
            • Map data is incomplete; routes do not know pavements or crossings.
            • Not a replacement for a cane, guide dog or human assistance.
            11. Online scene description (optional)
            • Tap Describe, or say "describe surroundings", to get a spoken description of one camera photo.
            • You can also type or say a question, such as "Is there a bus stop?".
            • The laptop sends that one photo to an online AI service (Google Gemini) and reads the answer aloud.
            • It is off until you allow it, needs internet on the laptop, and can be slow or unavailable.
            • The answer can be wrong. It never confirms that a path is safe, and walking guidance keeps working without it.
